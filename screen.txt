# PART 1: ONBOARDING FLOW (12 Screens)

---

## Screen 1: Splash Screen

**What the user sees:**

When a student opens the ACADIA app for the first time, they see a full-screen picture that fills their entire phone screen. On top of this picture, there is a dark see-through layer that makes the text easier to read. In the very center of the screen, there is a round container showing the ACADIA logo, which is a fire icon inside the outline of Ethiopia's map. Below the logo, the word "ACADIA" appears in big bold white letters. Under that, a smaller line of white text says "Empowering Ethiopian Students." At the very bottom of the screen, there is a thin navy blue loading bar that moves from left to right like a progress indicator, taking about three seconds to complete. If the admin has uploaded multiple splash images, small dot indicators at the bottom show which image is currently showing.

**What happens:**

The app starts up and connects to Firebase in the background. It checks if the user has saved login information from before. It also loads any splash images that the admin has set up from the app's internal storage (not from the internet, because many Ethiopian students use the app offline). If there are multiple images, they change every second with a smooth fade effect. After three seconds, the app looks at two things: first, whether the user is already logged in, and second, whether they have completed the initial setup (choosing their grade and stream). Based on this, three things can happen: if the user is logged in and has completed setup, they go straight to the Dashboard. If they have completed setup but are not logged in, they go to the Login screen. If they haven't completed setup yet, they go to the Welcome screen.

## Screen 2: Welcome Screen

**What the user sees:**

The top half of the phone screen has a picture slideshow that automatically changes between three images every few seconds. Small dots below the slideshow show which image you're currently looking at. Below the slideshow, big bold text says "Empowering Ethiopian Students Through Digital Education." Under that, the same message appears in Amharic: "የኢትዮጵያ ተማሪዎችን በዲጂታል ትምህርት ማብቃት." There is a large rounded button with a navy blue background that says "GET STARTED." Below this button, a blue underlined link says "Already a member? LOG IN." In the top-right corner, there is a question mark icon that opens a help tutorial. In the top-left corner, there is a globe icon that lets you switch between English, Amharic, and Afaan Oromoo languages.

**What the user can do:**

If the user taps the question mark icon, a popup appears with a dark overlay and a white card in the center. The card has the title "How to Use ACADIA" and four slides you can swipe through. The first slide shows "Browse Your Subjects" with a blue icon and a screenshot. The second slide shows "Purchase Your Grade" with a shopping cart icon. The third slide shows "Submit Payment" with a payment icon. The fourth slide shows "Start Learning" with a school icon. Four dots at the bottom show which slide you're on. There is a checkbox that says "Don't show again" which is already checked by default. A navy blue "GOT IT" button closes the popup. There is also a "Watch Video" option that opens a video tutorial with three language options.

The "GET STARTED" button takes the user to the Academic Path Selection screen. The "LOG IN" link takes the user to the Login screen.

## Screen 3: Academic Path Selection

**What the user sees:**

There is a back arrow in the top-left corner that returns to the Welcome screen. The top bar says "Choose Your Academic Path." At the top of the screen, a yellow warning pill with an exclamation mark says "This choice is PERMANENT" in bold amber text. Below this warning, there are two large cards. The first card has a school icon and says "HIGH SCHOOL" with subtitles "Grades 9 - 12" and "Natural and Social streams." The second card has a money/account icon and says "UNIVERSITY" with subtitles "Freshman - Senior" and "All generations and institutes."

When the user taps a card, it becomes highlighted with a navy blue border, a subtle shadow appears, and a navy blue checkmark circle appears in the top-right corner. Only one card can be selected at a time. At the bottom, there is a "CONTINUE" button that is grey and disabled until the user makes a selection. When a selection is made, the button becomes enabled with a navy blue gradient background.

**What happens:**

If the user selects HIGH SCHOOL and taps CONTINUE, they go to Grade Selection. If they select UNIVERSITY, they go to Generation Selection. The user's choice is stored permanently and cannot be changed later. After completing the entire academic path selection, the user will go to Registration, not directly to the Dashboard.

## Screen 4: Grade Selection (High School Path)

**What the user sees:**

This screen only appears for users who chose HIGH SCHOOL on the previous screen. There is a back arrow that returns to Academic Path Selection. The top bar says "Select Your Grade." Below that, a grey subtitle says "Choose your current grade level." Four large square cards are arranged in a 2x2 grid. Each card shows a large grade number (9, 10, 11, or 12) in big bold text, with the word "Grade" below it. Each grade has a slightly different shade of navy blue. If the user selects grade 11 or 12 and taps CONTINUE, they go to Stream Selection. If they select grade 9 or 10, the grade is saved and they go to Registration (not the Dashboard). The registration screen will ask for all required information including Google Sign-In and email/password options.

# PART 2: AUTHENTICATION (5 Screens)

---

## Screen 13: Register Screen

**What the user sees:**

There is a back arrow that returns to the previous screen. The header reads "Create Account." There is a button to sign in with Google. Below that, there are six form fields. The first field asks for "Full Name" with a person icon. The second field asks for "Phone Number" with a non-editable +251 prefix and a field for the remaining nine digits. The app automatically removes any zero if the user enters it before the 9 digits. The third field asks for "Email" with format validation. The fourth field asks for "Password" with a visibility toggle icon (eye) and text saying "Password must be at least 6 characters." The fifth field asks to "Confirm Password" with a visibility toggle icon. The sixth field asks for "High School Name" but only appears if the user selected the High School path during onboarding. Validation errors appear in red text below each field if something is wrong. A "REGISTER" button with a navy blue gradient and full width shows a loading spinner while the app processes the registration. It is disabled until all fields are valid. At the bottom, a link says "Already have an account? LOG IN" which takes the user to the Login screen.

**What happens:**

When the user fills in all fields and taps REGISTER, the app validates all information. It then sends the information to Firebase Auth to create the account. The academic path selected during onboarding is submitted with the registration data and permanently stored. The app sends an email verification email to the user. The user then goes to Email Verification screen.

**Important:** One account can only be used on one device at a time. If the user tries to log in on a new device, they will be asked to log out from the old device first.

---

## Screen 14: Login Screen

**What the user sees:**

There is a back arrow that returns to the previous screen. The header reads "Welcome Back." Users can also login with email option. An Email input field and a Password input field with visibility toggle icon appear. A "Forgot Password?" link in blue text takes the user to the Forgot Password screen. A "LOGIN" button with a navy blue gradient and full width shows a loading spinner during authentication. If the credentials are invalid, an error snackbar appears at the bottom. A link at the bottom says "Don't have an account? REGISTER" which takes the user to the Register screen. A support agent icon is shown in a support info box at the bottom.

**What happens:**

When the user enters their email and password and taps LOGIN, the app authenticates with Firebase. On success, the user goes to the Dashboard Home Tab. If the user is already logged in on another device, they will be prompted to log out from that device first.

## Screen 19: Home Tab

**What the user sees:**

This is the main landing page of the dashboard. At the top, there is a time-appropriate greeting using the user's first name (e.g., "Good Morning, Abebe!") in big bold text. Below that, the current date is displayed (e.g., "Monday, May 15, 2026"). To the right, there is a streak counter with a fire icon showing the current study streak in days inside an orange pill. This works offline and tracks real study sessions.

Next, there is a row of four small icon buttons called Quick Actions. The first one is a play icon labeled "Continue" - tapping this resumes the last viewed content. The second is a timer icon labeled "Study Time" - tapping this opens a timer with focus sessions. The third is an assignment icon labeled "Entrance" - tapping this goes to the Entrance tab (for Grades 11-12 only, or shows a coming soon message for others). The fourth is a download icon labeled "Downloads" - tapping this shows all downloaded content.

Below the Quick Actions, there is a section called "Your Subjects." There is a header row with "Your Subjects" in bold and a "See All" link. Below this, there is a 2x3 grid of subject cards. Each card has a navy blue top area, a custom JPG icon for the subject, the subject name in bold navy blue text, and small grey text saying "Tap to explore." When the user taps a subject, they go to the Subject Portal. When they tap "See All," a bottom sheet opens with all subjects in a 2-column grid.

At the bottom of the screen, there is a Daily Quote section. It has a gradient background and a quote icon. It displays one of 20 hardcoded educational quotes, with a new quote appearing each day.

## Screen 20: Subjects Tab

**What the user sees:**

This screen shows all subjects for the user's grade and stream. When the package is locked, there is an orange banner at the top that says "Grade X Stream Package - Locked" with the package name and an "UNLOCK - XXX ETB" button. Below this, there is a 2-column grid of subject cards. Each card has the subject's custom JPG icon, the subject name in navy blue text, the number of chapters, and a lock icon overlay showing that the subject is locked. All units and chapters are visible with their names but they remain locked.

When the package is unlocked, a navy blue banner at the top says "Package Unlocked" with validity information (e.g., "Valid until Jan 15, 2027"). Subject cards now show a thin colored progress bar instead of the lock icon, showing how much of the subject has been completed. Tapping an unlocked subject takes the user to the Subject Portal.

**Important:** Purchasing one subject means purchasing the whole package for that academic path. This unlocks all subjects under that path. The package is valid for one year from the approval date.

---

## Screen 21: Subject Portal

**What the user sees:**

The top bar shows the subject name with a back arrow. Below, there is a header card that displays the subject name with its custom JPG icon, the grade and stream path, and purchase validity text if purchased. The "CHAPTERS" section lists all chapter cards for that subject. Each card shows the unit number and title (e.g., "Unit 1: Introduction to Biology"), the content item count (e.g., "6 items"), and a colored horizontal progress bar showing completion. All chapters are accessible when the package is unlocked — there is no prerequisite locking. Tapping a chapter takes the user to the Chapter Content Screen.

---

## Screen 22: Chapter Content Screen

**What the user sees:**

A gradient header card at the top shows the subject name and chapter title. Below, six content type sections are listed vertically. Each section has a colored icon and header.

1. **Video** - Red play icon, "Video" header. Videos can be from YouTube but must not look like they're from YouTube, or they can be uploaded to GitHub releases.
2. **Short Note** - Blue description icon, "Short Note" header. These are PDF files from GitHub.
3. **Quiz** - Orange quiz icon, "Quiz" header. These are interactive MCQ JSON files from GitHub.
4. **Exam** - Purple assignment icon, "Exam" header. These are timed exam JSON files from GitHub.
5. **Flashcard** - Teal style icon, "Flashcard" header. These are flashcard JSON files from GitHub.
6. **Past Paper** - Brown folder icon, "Past Paper" header. These are interactive MCQ JSON files from GitHub.

**If content is uploaded:** Each section shows a card with the content title, metadata (like duration for videos, page count for PDFs, question count for quizzes/exams, card count for flashcards), and a DOWNLOAD button with a download icon. When the user taps DOWNLOAD, a circular progress indicator replaces the download button, showing download progress. After download completes, a green "OFFLINE" badge appears with a checkmark, and tapping opens the content in the appropriate viewer.

**If content is not uploaded:** The section shows a card with an hourglass icon and the text "Content will be uploaded soon by NextGen team" in grey italic text. This is the only placeholder text in the app — everything else is functional.

# PART 4: CONTENT VIEWERS (7 Screens)

---

## Screen 23: Video Player

**What the user sees:**

A full black immersive screen. The top bar shows the video title and a "Done" completion badge if the video has already been watched. The video plays full-width using the native video player. Standard media controls appear at the bottom: a draggable progress bar, current position and total duration timestamps, a rewind 10 seconds button, a large play/pause button in the center (in a navy blue circle), a forward 10 seconds button, and a volume toggle button. To the right of the controls, a speed selector allows choosing playback speed: 0.5x, 0.75x, 1.0x (normal), 1.25x, 1.5x, 2.0x. When paused, a large play icon overlay appears on the video. At the very bottom, a "Mark as Completed" button is available. When the video finishes playing, it auto-marks as completed. There is no YouTube branding, no external browser, no streaming — all playback is from the local downloaded file.

**What happens:**

The user watches the video, can control playback speed, rewind/forward, and mark it as completed. When completed, it's tracked in the user's progress.

---

## Screen 24: PDF Viewer

**What the user sees:**

The top bar shows the document title with a back arrow. Below the top bar, a document info bar displays "Page X of Y" on the left and the file size on the right. If the document is completed, a green "Completed" badge appears. The PDF is displayed full-screen with pinch-to-zoom enabled. The user can zoom in and out freely, and pan around the document. Zoom level percentage is shown in the top bar between zoom in and zoom out buttons. At the bottom, a navigation bar has left/right arrow buttons for page navigation, showing the current page and total pages. A "Mark as Completed" button with a navy blue checkmark is on the right.

**What happens:**

The user reads the PDF, can zoom in/out, navigate pages, and mark it as completed when finished.

---

## Screen 25: Quiz Screen

**What the user sees:**

The top bar shows "Quiz: [Title]" with a close button. Tapping the close button prompts a confirmation dialog warning that progress will not be saved. Below the top bar, a top status bar shows "Question X of Y" with a horizontal progress bar. The question area displays the question text in bold 18-pixel text inside a white card with a small primary-colored "Question X" pill at the top. Below the question, four multiple-choice options labeled A, B, C, and D are displayed as rounded rectangular containers. Each has a circular letter indicator on the left and the option text. Tapping an option selects it — the selected option gets a colored border, the letter circle fills with the primary color, and a checkmark icon appears on the right.

**In Immediate Feedback Mode:** When the user taps an answer, correct choices turn green with a green checkmark. Wrong choices turn red, and the correct answer highlights green. A "Show Explanation" button appears below the options. Tapping it reveals the explanation text in a light grey box.

**In Review at End Mode:** The user answers all questions without feedback. At the end, they see all their answers marked correct (green) or wrong (red) with explanations.

At the bottom, navigation buttons "PREV" and "NEXT" allow moving between questions. On the last question, the NEXT button changes to a "FINISH" button. There is no separate submit step — results are calculated automatically.

**What happens:**

The user answers questions, gets feedback (depending on mode), and when finished, goes to Quiz Result Screen.

---

## Screen 26: Quiz Result Screen

**What the user sees:**

A large circular score indicator (200-pixel diameter) in the center shows the score as a fraction (e.g., "7/10") in 48-pixel bold text and the percentage (e.g., "70%") in 22-pixel text. The circle border is green if passed (≥60%) or red if failed. Below, a pass/fail message appears in large bold text. Three stat cards in a horizontal row display: Correct count with green check icon and percentage, Wrong count with red cancel icon and percentage, and Time taken with navy blue timer icon and minutes:seconds. A details card shows the quiz name, subject, chapter, total questions, passing score, and the user's score highlighted in the primary color. A "View Answers & Explanations" outlined button appears showing full answers with descriptions. If the user failed, a "Retry Quiz" orange button appears. A navy blue gradient "Back to Dashboard" button is at the bottom. If passed, an achievement banner with a trophy icon says "Achievement Unlocked! Quiz Master: Passed your quiz."

**What happens:**

The user sees their results, can review answers with explanations, retry if failed, or return to dashboard.

---

## Screen 27: Exam Screen

**What the user sees:**

Similar to the Quiz screen but with a countdown timer bar at the very top. The timer shows remaining time in large bold digits with a timer icon. The bar background changes color: normal is grey, under 5 minutes turns orange, under 1 minute turns red. A "Pause" button sits next to the timer. When paused, the entire question area is hidden and replaced with an orange banner saying "Exam is paused. Questions are hidden. Tap Resume to continue." A progress bar shows "Question X of Y" with percentage. Below the question and options, a question navigator grid appears — small numbered squares for every question. Answered questions have a green border, the current question is filled with the primary color, unanswered questions are grey. Tapping a number jumps to that question. At the bottom, "PREV", "SUBMIT EXAM", and "NEXT" buttons. Submitting with unanswered questions triggers a confirmation dialog warning that unanswered questions will be marked as wrong. When the timer reaches zero, the exam auto-submits.

**What happens:**

The user takes a timed exam, can pause, navigate between questions, and when finished or time runs out, goes to Exam Result Screen.

---

## Screen 28: Exam Result Screen

**What the user sees:**

Similar to Quiz Result but with the addition of an unanswered count stat card (orange) if any questions were left unanswered. The score circle, stats cards, details card, action buttons, and achievement banner follow the same layout. Full answers with descriptions are shown in the final results for both quiz and exam.

**What happens:**

The user sees their exam results, can review answers with explanations, retry if failed, or return to dashboard.

---

## Screen 29: Flashcard Screen

**What the user sees:**

The top bar shows "Flashcards: [Title]" with the mastered count displayed on the right (e.g., "5/20"). A progress bar shows "Card X of Y". The center of the screen is dominated by a large flashcard with rounded corners, a white background, and a subtle shadow. On the front, a question mark icon appears at the top, and the question text is displayed in large bold 22-pixel text centered. Below the text, a label says "QUESTION" in small grey uppercase letters. At the very bottom of the card, small text reads "TAP TO FLIP". When the user taps the card, a smooth 3D flip animation rotates the card to reveal the back. The back has a lightbulb icon, the answer text in 18-pixel text, and an "ANSWER" label. Tapping again flips back to the question. Below the card, two action buttons sit side by side: "DIDN'T KNOW" in red outlined style, and "GOT IT!" in navy blue filled style. Tapping "GOT IT!" marks the card as mastered and advances to the next card. Tapping "DIDN'T KNOW" keeps the card in the active deck and advances. When all cards are reviewed, a completion dialog appears showing the total reviewed, mastered count, and cards needing more practice. Options to restart or finish are provided.

**What happens:**

The user reviews flashcards, flips them, marks them as mastered or not, and tracks progress. When all cards are done, they see a completion summary.
